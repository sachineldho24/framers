## PosterX V2: Full Technical Plan

This is a genuinely exciting idea, and your instinct about the Canva opportunity is right. But you need to know a few important realities about how Canva's APIs actually work before choosing your path.

---

### Understanding the Canva API Landscape First

Canva offers developers two primary pathways for integration: the **Canva Apps SDK** and the **Canva Connect APIs**. The Apps SDK enables you to build applications that function directly *within* the Canva editor, while the Connect APIs allow you to integrate Canva's functionality *into your own platforms* via REST APIs.

Here's the critical thing you need to understand about both:

**Canva Connect API** — You can use the Connect APIs to create web app integrations that create and sync assets, designs, comments, and more. However, a private integration (what you'd need for a personal store) is only available to your team on a **Canva Enterprise plan**. Public integrations must first be reviewed and approved by Canva before they're available to all users. Enterprise plans are expensive and overkill for what you need.

**The "Embed" Approach** — There is a Canva embed endpoint that allows external websites to launch a Canva editing window inside a popup or iframe. This is exactly how many POD websites and marketing platforms allow users to edit templates. However, Canva doesn't provide public documentation for this feature, and since it isn't officially documented, updates may break your code. Autosaving, template locking, and white-label features require partnership permission.

**Bottom line:** There's no clean, officially-supported, plug-it-into-WordPress API for embedding the Canva *editor* on your site. What most businesses actually do is more clever — and it's what I'd recommend too.

---

### The Right Architecture: 3 Paths Ranked

#### ✅ Path 1 (Recommended): WooCommerce + Canva Deep Link Flow

This is the most practical and fastest-to-launch approach. You don't embed Canva — instead you *redirect* users into Canva with a pre-configured template, and they return to your checkout with the exported design.

**How the user flow works:**

1. User lands on your product page (e.g., "A3 Birthday Frame")
2. They click "Design Your Print" — this opens Canva in a new tab with a *pre-built template* that's already the exact right size (you create these once)
3. User designs in Canva normally, then exports/downloads their design as a high-res PDF or PNG
4. They return to your WooCommerce product page, upload the file using a product customization plugin
5. They proceed to WooCommerce checkout with the file attached to the order

**WooCommerce plugins that handle the file-upload-to-order piece:**
- **Product Add-Ons (WooCommerce official)** — lets customers upload a file at checkout, which gets attached to every order and visible in your admin dashboard
- **YITH WooCommerce Product Add-Ons** — similar, very polished UI

**Why this works well:**
- WooCommerce handles 100% of the order management, tracking, and checkout you already want
- No API agreements, no Enterprise Canva plans needed
- You create size-specific Canva templates once and link to them per product
- Your admin dashboard is just the native WooCommerce orders panel

**The one friction point:** Users leave your site briefly to design, then come back. This is a real UX trade-off — but it's also what most print shops currently do, and users are already comfortable with it since they know Canva.

---

#### ⚡ Path 2 (Best Long-Term): Canva Add-on (Apps SDK)

This is actually your **"Secondary Idea"** from your brief, but I'd argue it's the strongest product play of the three. Here's why it's special:

A Canva App is a JavaScript app that runs inside an iframe within the Canva editor's side panel. The app can interact with the editor via injected APIs — including the `requestExport` method, which lets your app trigger a design export programmatically.

**What your add-on would do:**
1. User is designing a birthday frame (or any poster) inside Canva
2. They open your "PosterX" app from the Canva Apps sidebar
3. The app shows: frame size options, quantity, delivery details, price
4. They click "Order Print" — your app calls `requestExport`, grabs the design as a high-res file, and sends the order directly to your backend
5. Payment happens either in-app (via a redirect to your payment page) or through a checkout link that opens in a new tab

The Apps SDK gives you access to a Content Query API, Design Editing API, Rich Text API, and AI capabilities for apps that operate within Canva. The export trigger is the key piece you need.

**Why this is powerful:** You're capturing customers where they already are — inside Canva designing things. You're not asking them to come to your site. That's a massive distribution advantage.

**The catch:** Building and publishing a Canva App requires going through Canva's review/approval process for the Apps Marketplace. It takes time. But once it's live, you're in front of millions of Canva users organically.

---

#### 🔧 Path 3: Fully Custom Build

Only go here if you want total control and are willing to invest significantly in development. For a one-person operation, the maintenance overhead is high. You'd need:

- A custom React/Next.js frontend with the embedded Canva iframe (using the undocumented embed endpoint)
- A Node.js or FastAPI backend for orders
- A database (PostgreSQL) for order tracking
- Payment integration (Razorpay for India, or Stripe)
- An admin dashboard (you could use something like AdminJS or build it yourself)

Given your stack (FastAPI, Python, React), you *could* build this — but it's months of work vs. weeks for Path 1.
